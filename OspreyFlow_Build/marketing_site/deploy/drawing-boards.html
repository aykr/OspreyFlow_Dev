Drawing boards

Drawing boards: declare a node, draw a link

Field analysts build a network by declaring a person, an account or a place and drawing the links between them. OspreyFlow has a drawing board for this, kept apart from loaded data, with every drawn item attributed, marked, graded and sourced.

Built, with parts still coming. Boards, drawing, the case's analyst layer, grading, pictures, matches, your own link types and promotion into a dataset are built. An alert when new data meets a drawn item, and drawn items on the analysis canvas, are still to come. Each section is marked Built or Coming. Screenshots follow.

BuiltWhy a board, and not the analysis canvas

The analysis canvas shows what the data says. A drawing board is where an analyst writes down what they know that the data does not yet say. The two are kept apart on purpose, and the analysis canvas never draws.

  • Open it blank, from a view, or from a selection. A board starts empty, from a view of the case, or from the items you selected, which come as live references to the loaded data.
  • Always inside a case. A board belongs to a case. A drawing begun as a case of its own makes its own dataset, its case and its board.
  • Yours until you share it. A new board is its creator's alone until shared with the case, and only its creator draws on it.
  • ComingDrawn items on the analysis canvas, dashed and read-only, with an Edit on board action.

BuiltDeclare, draw and move

Moving and linking are different gestures, so neither is done by accident.

  • Declare a node. Its type comes from the dataset's domain model. If the type you need is missing: Need another type? Ask a data engineer.
  • Dragging an item moves it, and so do the arrow keys.
  • Drawing a link is done by dragging an item's link handle onto another item, by holding Alt and dragging the item, or by right-clicking and choosing Draw a link from here.
  • By keyboard. A Draw a link button is the keyboard route. Every board action works without a pointer, and a keyboard test proves each one.
  • Withdraw, never delete. You edit your own items and withdraw them. A withdrawn item is kept in the record, not erased.
  • Pictures. Attach an uploaded image or an image document already on the board. An uploaded image carries its own marking and source, and a reader not cleared for it sees no picture.

BuiltA case's own analyst layer

What you draw is not written into the source dataset. Each case has its own analyst layer, read together with the case's source data.

  • Source data stays clean. A drawn link can join a drawn item to a loaded one. The link lives in the analyst layer, and the loaded item is untouched.
  • Nothing leaks to other cases. No other case sees a drawn item, and none can infer it from a degree, a path length, a count or a chart bar. Access is applied in the store, as for any dataset, with an access test on each read path.
  • Nothing is merged. A drawn item that resembles a loaded one is never merged with it.
  • Watched. Drawing an item sets a watch on it, and withdrawing the item ends the watch.

BuiltMarked, graded and sourced

A drawn item is a person's assertion, and it says so: drawn dashed and in amber on the board.

  • Who, where and how sure. Each item records who drew it, on which board and case, its handling marking, a grading in your deployment's scheme, and a source note (free text, a document the case holds, or both).
  • A marking ceiling. Each dataset has a highest allowed marking. An item, an edit or a picture marked above it is refused, in words.
  • The grading scheme. One setting for the deployment, 5x5x5 by default or 3x5x2. Changing it is a deliberate command with a dry run first; every item keeps its original grade beside the new one.

BuiltLooks like a loaded item

When a drawn item resembles something already loaded, the board offers the pair.

  • The analyst decides. Same keeps a link in the case's analyst layer; Not the same records the opposite. Neither changes the loaded item.
  • A suggestion stays a suggestion. The offer never acts on its own. There is no automatic face matching.

BuiltYour own link types

When no link type fits, an analyst can define a new one, saying why none fits.

  • Usable at once, marked proposed, in that case.
  • A data engineer decides. The proposal goes to the Ingest Studio's Model tab, where a data engineer of the dataset adds it to the draft domain model or declines it. Its proposer never decides it.

BuiltPromotion into a dataset, as a load

When drawn items are ready to join the shared data, Promote… lists them, the case's Author ticks what goes, and the whole set goes into the case's dataset as one load.

  • Checked before it goes, and again as it goes. Each marking against the dataset's highest allowed marking, each type against its model, and each item for its grade. A link goes only when both its ends will be in the dataset. Anything left out is named, with the reason.
  • Per dataset, author promotes or a second person reviews. Set in Admin, with a warning when fewer than two people could approve. The review is in the Ingest Studio, by one of the dataset's Authors who may review promotions, never the person who sent it; nothing reaches the dataset before it is approved. Promotions waiting too long are flagged to the dataset's Authors and administrators.
  • Still marked as drawn. A promoted item keeps its drawn-by-an-analyst mark, its marking and its grading. A promotion is taken back like any load, and every promotion and decision is audited.
  • A removed case's layer. When a case is removed, the dataset's Authors merge what it drew into the dataset, as a promotion, or delete it. Nothing deletes it on its own.

ComingAn alert when new data meets a drawn item

After a load into a source dataset, anything that resembles a drawn item in a case reading that dataset is designed to raise one alert in that case, with an Open the board action, in the one alert queue of monitoring and alerting. Nothing is merged. The watch each drawn item sets is built; its alerts are still to come.

  • Every theme. Each board screen is built for the dark, light and GOV.UK themes, and checked with automated accessibility scans in all three.

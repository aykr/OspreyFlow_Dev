What "designed to meet" means

OspreyFlow is designed to meet the GDS Service Standard. Three independent checks are planned and not yet done: an independent accessibility audit, a penetration test and a data protection impact assessment (DPIA). Only a service assessment panel decides whether a service meets the Standard, and it looks at a service, not at a product alone.

The GOV.UK theme, chosen at install

The GOV.UK theme is a deployment setting, not a separate edition or a fork. One setting gives every page the GOV.UK header and footer, the phase banner and the service's own name. Everything else is the same product, with the same features and the same tests.

  • For services on a gov.uk domain. The GOV.UK look belongs to those services, and is to be used for those alone. It stays available on one computer for a demonstration.
  • Your service's name, not ours. The install asks for the service's name, the organisation and the phase.
  • Switch back at any time. Nothing about the data or the cases changes.
  • The four places in the GOV.UK header, with the banner as a GOV.UK notification banner.

What a security or architecture team will ask

Where does the data live?

Inside your own cloud account or data centre. The deployment does not call out on its own: the install downloads its images and model, a server fetches its own certificate, and anything else, such as a hosted model or your organisation's sign-in, is switched on by an administrator.

Who can see what?

Handling markings on every record, clearance and unit on every person, and reads filtered in the store, so a hidden record leaves no trace in a count.

Can a finding be traced?

Every link keeps its source, its record and when it arrived. Every export and approval is in the audit.

What about models?

Local by default. A hosted engine receives material only when the dataset and the handling code both allow it.

Is it accessible?

Designed to WCAG 2.2 AA, with automated accessibility scans on every screen we test, in the light and dark themes and under GOV.UK on most of them, and a Matrix view of the network as tables. An independent audit is planned.

What is inside it?

Open-source components throughout, each listed with its licence. No software licence fee.

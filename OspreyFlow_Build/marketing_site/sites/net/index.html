Intelligence mapping · from Solirius Reply

See the whole picture. Own the platform it runs on.

OspreyFlow joins records about people, places, objects and events into one network your analysts can explore, question and act on. It is open source, with no software licence fee, and runs inside your own cloud or data centre.

The problem: the picture is in pieces

Five sources, five tools

Call records in spreadsheets, intelligence reports in another system, a network drawn by hand, pins on a separate map, watch lists somewhere else.

What goes wrong

Links are missed between tools and teams. The chain back to the source breaks in every copy. Access is set up five times, five ways.

What it costs

Analysts spend their weeks preparing data. Findings are hard to defend when they are challenged. Figures for leadership are typed in again by hand.

One platform, one picture

OspreyFlow loads each source through a pipeline, resolves the same person across them and shows the result as one network, read as a graph, a map, a timeline, tables and reports. A filter in one view applies in all of them.

  • Monitoring and alerting built in: rules written as sentences, hotlists and screening lists.
  • Questions to a model, answered from the case and citing what they drew on.
  • Dashboards for leadership, from the same figures.

Why organisations choose it

Defensible

Every link shows where it came from. A model's suggestion stays inferred until a person confirms it. A match can be undone.

Yours

Apache-2.0 open source. You own the data, the deployment and the roadmap you ask of it. No per-seat licence.

Inside your boundary

Runs in your own cloud account or data centre. Nothing leaves unless an administrator enables it for a dataset.

Need-to-know by design

Access is enforced in the store, so a record someone may not see leaves no trace in a count, a path or a chart.

For the UK public sector

OspreyFlow includes a dedicated configuration for UK government: the GOV.UK Design System's look, government security markings with need-to-know access, and a full audit trail.

OspreyFlow is designed to meet the GDS Service Standard. Three independent checks are planned and not yet done: an independent accessibility audit, a penetration test and a data protection impact assessment (DPIA). Only a service assessment panel decides whether a service meets the Standard, and it looks at a service, not at a product alone.
